# Round briefs

Templates for the two `delegate_task` rounds. Fill the angle brackets. Keep
the problem statement byte-identical across every task in a round, so the only
difference between children is their seat.

## Round 1 — one task per seat

```
You are one of several independent reviewers. You cannot see the others.

PROBLEM
<the problem statement: question, the decision it informs, facts,
constraints, what a good answer must cover>

YOUR ANGLE
<seat name>: <the question this seat attacks from>

ANSWER FORMAT
1. Your answer in 3-6 sentences.
2. Each supporting claim on its own line, tagged [evidence: <source, file,
   figure or observation>] or [assumption].
3. The single assumption that, if false, would most change your answer.
4. What you would check next, and what result would change your mind.
Do not hedge to sound balanced. Take a position.
```

Do not give a child the owner's identity, credentials or private context it
does not need. Children can read and research with their own tools; say so in
the brief if the question needs fresh data.

## Round 2 — one task per seat, the others' answers only

```
Below are answers from other independent reviewers to the same problem,
labelled by letter. You did not write any of them.

PROBLEM
<the same problem statement>

ANSWERS
[A] <answer text, seat name removed>
[B] <answer text, seat name removed>
...

YOUR JOB — attack only
For each answer, list its weakest points: unsupported claims, missing cases,
unpriced costs, contradictions with another answer. Name the letter and the
claim each time. Do not propose your own answer and do not summarise.
If an answer survives your attack, say what you checked and why it held.
End with: one question none of the answers addressed.
```

Leave out the child's own round-1 answer. Shuffle the letters between tasks so
no answer always sits first.

## Failure checks before you judge

| What you see | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Every round-1 answer agrees | The seats were not really different, or the framing pushed one answer | Check the briefs for a leading phrase; note the agreement as weak, or rerun with sharper angles |
| Round 2 is polite | The brief invited summary instead of attack | Rerun once with the attack-only brief; if still polite, report the round as weak |
| Answers full of "consider" and "may" | Nobody took a position | Treat them as no answer; do not let hedges become findings |
| Your verdict lists steps and owners | The judgement turned into a plan | Cut it back to findings and hand it to planning |
| One seat's answer dominates your summary | You were persuaded by length, not evidence | Re-read the shortest well-evidenced answer |

## Report shape

```
Question: <one line>
Panel: <N> seats (<angles>), <models if varied>, <2N> child runs
Agreements: <point> — independent evidence | shared assumption
Disagreements: <point> — side A strongest case / side B strongest case / check that settles it
Knocked down: <claim> — by <critique>
Nobody checked: <question>
Verdict: <your call> — because <reasons>
Limits: <shared model, your framing, anything that failed>
```
